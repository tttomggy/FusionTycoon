# Make the orb the star of Fusion Tycoon

On Roblox in 2025-2026, the image that wins attention is one big, bright, easy-to-read subject that clearly shows the game's main action. It is usually the thing players collect, not a person. It should read at about 150 px, keep text short and off the bottom edge, and be honest about the first few minutes of play. For a new game, the most important image is the **16:9 Home thumbnail**, not the icon. Since April 2024, Roblox's main discovery feed (Recommended for You) has used thumbnails, and Roblox can A/B test them for you through Thumbnail Personalization. That feature averaged **+8.5% qualified play-through rate**, and some games gained 50%. Icons still appear in Search, Charts, Continue and Favorites, but they can't be tested. Roblox ranks games on play-through rate together with how fast new players leave, so a thumbnail that gets clicks but oversells hurts you twice. The chart leaders back this up. Steal a Brainrot, Grow a Garden, Pet Simulator 99 and 99 Nights in the Forest put a **collectible or creature** at the centre, not the developer's avatar, and several mega-hits have very simple icons that work because the title already explains the game. For Fusion Tycoon, that means: make a glowing, mutated orb the mascot, show a blocky generic character reacting to it, rotate 4-5 thumbnails that each sell a different hook (fusing, mutations, stealing, events, a full lab), add a bracketed event emoji to the title, and judge everything on qualified play-through rate and early bounce, not on which image you like best.

## Your thumbnail is now the main ad, and Roblox tests it for free

Since April 2024, Roblox's Home recommendations have shown a **16:9 thumbnail**. They use the first image in your Home Page tab, or a personalised pick from your active set. The 1:1 icon stays in "the Continue, Sponsored, and Favorite sections of Home, Search, and Discover." Roblox said the change gave **"a 7.2% increase in overall unique plays from recommendations"**. In February 2025 it reached new mobile and tablet users too ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/169-experience-thumbnails-are-coming-to-home-in-april-%E2%80%93-update-yours-now/2875237)). Home matters most because, in Roblox's words, it "is usually where the vast majority of users find games" ([Roblox Docs: Acquisition](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/analytics/acquisition)). Two caveats. The 2024 post's statement about Sponsored is already out of date: current Ads Manager docs say ad creatives are 16:9 thumbnails ([Roblox Docs: Ads Manager](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/ads-manager)). And in January 2026 Roblox began testing thumbnails instead of icons in search for a small group of users ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/game-searches-are-showing-the-thumbnail-instead-of-the-icon/4292368)). The thumbnail keeps taking over more of the icon's job.

The specs are simple. Thumbnails are **1920×1080, 16:9, under 3 MB**. Any other shape gets stretched, not cropped. You can have up to 10 images or videos on the game page. The icon is one square of at least **512×512** per language, and it often shows at about 150×150, so Roblox tells you to preview it small. The only official safe-area rule: **keep important text and art out of the bottom of the thumbnail**, because the player count covers it ([Roblox Docs: Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails); [Roblox Docs: Icons](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/experience-icons)). Roblox gives no exact size for that zone. Treating the bottom 15-20%, and especially the bottom-right, as a no-text zone is a sensible precaution.

**Thumbnail Personalization** is the built-in tester. It isn't a plain 50/50 A/B split. Roblox shows each active thumbnail (2-5 of them) to random users, works out which one gets the best **Qualified Play-Through Rate (QPTR)** for each user group, and sends more traffic to the winner while keeping a little on the others. QPTR is qualified plays divided by Home-recommendation impressions. A "qualified play" is a "meaningful play session" that "filters out accidental clicks or quick bounces." Results start appearing after "a few hours." Roblox advises keeping several thumbnails active instead of picking one winner, testing new ones with each major update, and then leaving them alone until the next update ([Roblox Docs: Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails); [Roblox Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery)). Since July 2025, a new test keeps your current winner's share of traffic instead of resetting to an even split, and staff say to **"always include your highest QPTR existing thumbnail when starting a new test"** ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/thumbnail-personalization-now-remembers-your-existing-winning-thumbnails/3793665)). It isn't perfect. Developers have reported the system favouring a lower-QPTR thumbnail, and impressions falling even after a better thumbnail won ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/thumbnail-personalization-consistently-picking-worst-performing-qptr-thumbnail-is-this-a-bug/3272087); [DevForum, Feb 2026](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/big-impressions-drop-despite-new-thumbnails-having-a-higher-qptr/4337114)). It also only measures Home-recommendation traffic, so a brand-new game with few impressions will collect data slowly.

There is **no native way to A/B test the icon**. A feature request from November 2024 was still getting replies in July 2026 ("Just an icon alone can make a difference in D1 stats") ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/icon-ab-testing/3274267)). The usual workaround: check the icon carefully before shipping, change it only together with an update, and change one thing at a time so the analytics annotation shows what moved ([creatorxp.gg](https://creatorxp.gg/guides/roblox-game-icon-mistakes)).

## Ranking rewards honest clicks, so clickbait backfires

To decide how far a game spreads, Recommended for You uses only players who **came from Recommended for You**. Its "most important" signals are **play-through rate**, **first-play bounce rate** (a negative signal, measured for sessions under 60 seconds and 61-180 seconds), play days per user and playtime per user ([Roblox Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery)). The thumbnail drives the first signal directly and the second indirectly. Roblox says plainly that "inaccurate descriptions lead to bounces, which hurts your signals" ([Discovery FAQ](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery-faq)). It also gives the reverse example: a dinosaur thumbnail on a generic obby is "not recommended to users." The signals are per-user averages tested daily over a moving window, so a small game isn't penalised for being small, and a bad start doesn't follow you forever. Content updates set off an "explore" spike of new users, which makes the update the best time to test new art.

The content rules are short but strict. Anything implying money ("Robux! Play now!") is **not prioritised for recommendations**. Titles and images that copy an existing game lose discoverability. Roblox credits whichever game used the look first ([Roblox Docs: Discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery); [Discovery FAQ](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery-faq)). The Community Standards ban "deceptive, sensational, or otherwise misleading content or metadata" ([Roblox Community Standards](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/203313410-Roblox-Rules-of-Conduct)). Sponsored ads also ban fake buttons and notifications, "free Robux" offers, QR codes and links ([Advertising Standards](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/13722260778260-Advertising-Standards)). Video thumbnails have the strictest written rules: real gameplay only, no "artificially enhancing graphics beyond what a player will experience," no voice-over, and overlay text only to describe gameplay ("Collect coins to boost jumps"), never "50% off" or "Free UGC!" ([Roblox Docs: Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails)). For still images, no rule names the Robux symbol directly. Still, a Robux icon, "FREE" or "UGC" in the art clearly falls under "implies monetary reward." Image moderation can also be inconsistent: one developer got an account warning for a thumbnail with guns while the same image passed as an icon ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/thumbnail-got-me-a-warning-while-icon-didnt/2643599)).

This creates a real tension for Fusion Tycoon. Stealing doesn't unlock until **Rebirth 1**, and your own economy sim puts that at about **1 hour 4 minutes** for a free player. Secret-tier orbs are a 0.002% gacha pull or a Rebirth-1 fusion. A thumbnail that sells stealing or a Secret orb promises something a new player can't do in their first session. One icon guide says to list everything the art promises and check that each thing is reachable **within 60 seconds of spawning** ([creatorxp.gg](https://creatorxp.gg/guides/roblox-game-icon-mistakes)). The steal concept is still worth testing, because the heist is visible from minute one (other players carry orbs, there are red hand markers, and the "Unlocks at Rebirth 1" teaser). But it is the concept most likely to raise early bounce. That is why the plan below judges it on average playtime, not just QPTR.

## The 2025-26 hits sell the collectible, not the developer

The chart leaders are clear. In Q2 2025 the top games by average concurrent players were led by Grow a Garden (1.39M), Brookhaven, Dead Rails, Steal a Brainrot (356K), Blox Fruits and 99 Nights in the Forest. Simulation made up **22% of the top 100** ([MaxPower Gaming](https://www.maxpowergaming.co/post/from-gardens-to-brainrot-the-most-popular-roblox-games-of-q2-2025)). A June 2026 snapshot from an aggregator I couldn't fully verify lists idle and RNG risers such as Sell Lemons and Kick a Lucky Block, and concludes that "simulation and idle/RNG formats dominate" ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/the-most-popular-roblox-games-right-now-ranked-by-live-players)). Steal a Brainrot, the closest match to Fusion Tycoon's steal loop, peaked at **25.4M concurrent players**. Its collectibles are **voxel versions of Italian brainrot meme characters**, and its viral fuel was clips of kids upset after losing their brainrots ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). Grow a Garden was built by a 16-year-old in three days. About **35% of its players are under 13**, and it keeps them with weekly exclusives and themed updates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)).

I could not view any of these games' current icons directly, so I can't describe their pixels with a source. What the research does show: no current top game was found leading with the developer's own avatar. The recognisable "character" is the **collectible or creature itself**: the brainrot, the pet, the plant, the 99 Nights deer. Fans recreating 99 Nights art ask for "the deer… standing behind the 2 survivors" ([Gimkit forum](https://forum.creative.gimkit.com/t/thumbnail-request-for-99-nights-in-the-forest-no-due-date/201045)). Developers also note that Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, Miner's Haven and MM2 have "basic if not simplistic logo designs" and did well anyway ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rovisuals-high-ctr-thumbnails-game-icons-more-using-ai/4346638?page=2)). The likely reason: their names are a verb plus a noun that states the whole loop, so the art only has to show that verb happening to that noun. One 2026 chart hit's first thumbnail was taken apart on the forum. It had about six elements (a map screenshot, a cut-out character, overlay effects), all doable "in an hour at max" with Studio and Canva (same thread). That is good news if you're working alone. Clarity beats polish.

So leaving your avatar out costs nothing. It matches what the leaders do. Character art is still useful as a *reaction*. Artist and blog sources say stylised faces draw the eye ("faces consistently outperform scenic shots"), but that claim comes without a source ([RoWatcher, Sep 2026](https://rowatcher.com/news/the-roblox-thumbnail-is-a-conversion-ad-stop-designing-it-like-art)). The standard artist method is a generic blocky rig posed in Blender with an exaggerated face ([DevForum GFX tutorial](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-make-a-gfx/1728937)). A plain R6/R15 "everykid" or the red thief rig you already built for the HOW TO HEIST scenes does that job without being anyone's real avatar. One warning about copying the brainrot look: copycats are so common that Steal a Brainrot's developers filed at least four lawsuits after November 2025 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-24/roblox-s-hit-game-steal-a-brainrot-battles-its-many-imitators)), and Roblox's own systems down-rank look-alike metadata. Copy the structure (a collectible people want, one clear verb, one reaction), not the characters or the title format.

## Composition, colour and text: craft rules with little proof behind them

Every non-Roblox source repeats the same craft rules: **one large, close-cropped subject**, a simple or blurred background, strong contrast, at most about five elements, and a test at 128-150 px on a real phone. One guide says to show the icon to five people who don't know the game and ask them what genre it is ([creatorxp.gg](https://creatorxp.gg/guides/roblox-game-icon-mistakes); [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/the-roblox-thumbnail-is-a-conversion-ad-stop-designing-it-like-art)). The most common icon mistakes are not showing the genre, art that is "impressive but says nothing," detail that disappears at 128 px, art that disagrees with the title, and promising more than the first minute delivers. A moody, cinematic hero "in a smoky environment" is called out by name as a way to hide your genre ([creatorxp.gg](https://creatorxp.gg/guides/roblox-game-icon-mistakes)). Forum critiques of weak icons keep saying the same things: make the subject bigger, cut the empty space, make outline colours match, and blur or vignette the background ([DevForum, 2025](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-my-game-icon/3962887); [DevForum, 2022](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/is-this-game-icon-really-that-bad-game-only-got-0012-sponsoring-ctr/2038721)).

On colour, Roblox's own guidance is "bright, high-saturation colors for a fantasy or dreamy setting" and expressing the theme through colour ([Roblox Docs: Icons](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/experience-icons)). One blog argues that warm reds, oranges and yellows stand out more than cool colours on Roblox's dark home page, but gives no evidence ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/the-roblox-thumbnail-is-a-conversion-ad-stop-designing-it-like-art)). That matters for you, because a neon lab tends to come out teal and purple. The fix is contrast inside the image: a warm gold or rainbow orb blazing against the cool lab. For text, the advice is **three words or fewer, in capitals, thick outline, at least ~40 px tall in the source image**, naming a mechanic or reward rather than repeating the game title (same source). Watch out for the "20-40% CTR lift" figures in these blogs. They have no named source, and the sites sell services. The only well-measured numbers in this whole topic are Roblox's own: +8.5% average QPTR, up to +50%, and +7.2% plays from the 16:9 switch. Public developer CTR numbers are almost all sponsored-ad results from 0.01% to 1.1%, mixed up with budget and targeting changes ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/ctr-and-play-rate-took-a-nosedive-after-improving-icons-and-thumbnails/3099908)). Treat the craft rules as a strong starting point, and let your own personalization data decide.

Cost isn't the blocker. Talent Hub and Discord artists charge about **$10 (R$1,000) per icon and $20 (R$2,000) per thumbnail** at the low end ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/open-pieonrs-gfx-commisions/3899016)). You can also capture scenes yourself with Studio's free camera (Left Shift + P in a solo playtest) and finish them in Photopea ([Roblox Docs: Thumbnails](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. It isn't "what art do kids click?" but "what *true* promise gets a kid to click and then stay?" Roblox's ranking makes those the same question. Fusion Tycoon's strongest honest promise is the one a new player gets in their first minute: pull a glowing orb, see a mutation, fuse two into something bigger. The steal hook is your most viral idea (Steal a Brainrot proved loss-and-revenge clips travel), but it's also your most delayed one, so treat it as a test, not the lead image. Not using your own avatar isn't a handicap. The hits made their collectible the mascot, and your orbs, with seven mutation looks and a 119-entry Index, are already a mascot system. The one thing to avoid is borrowing brainrot characters or "Steal a ___" naming. Roblox down-ranks look-alikes, and the original's owners are suing copycats.

One thing remains unknown: none of the top games' current icons could be checked by eye for this report. Before you finalise, spend ten minutes on roblox.com/charts on your phone and look at the simulator and tycoon rows. Note which ones you can read at a glance, and design to stand out from them, not to blend in.

## Recommended plan

**Icon (one image, can't be A/B tested, so check it before shipping).** A single huge orb fills about 70% of the square, slightly off-centre, in **Rainbow or Golden** so it reads warm against a dark violet-to-teal neon lab background that is blurred and vignetted. Give it a thick dark outline and a soft rim light so the silhouette survives at 150 px. Add one small storytelling touch: a blocky gloved hand reaching in from the edge (hints at stealing), *or* a second orb half-merged into it with a bright spark seam (hints at fusing). Pick one, not both. Use no text, or at most one outlined word ("FUSE!") at the top. The game name already sits under the icon. Don't add faces to the orbs unless orbs have faces in the game, because the art has to match. Before shipping, view it at 128 px on your phone next to the chart leaders and run the five-stranger "what genre is this?" test. Ship it with an update, and write the date down so you can compare the Search and Continue numbers before and after.

**Thumbnails (activate 4-5 in Thumbnail Personalization; keep text in the top two-thirds and the bottom ~20% clear).**

| # | Concept | Focal subject and reaction | Text (top, ≤3 words) | What it tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fusion burst** (lead) | Two orbs slamming into the Fusion Machine; a much larger glowing orb bursting out; a generic blocky kid with a shocked open mouth | "FUSE TO SECRET" | The core loop: is fusing the hook? |
| 2 | **Mutation line-up** | One orb shown big in Golden ×2 → Diamond ×5 → Rainbow ×12 → Celestial ×20, rising left to right like a staircase | "RAINBOW ×12!" | Collecting and rarity: the Index and gacha appeal |
| 3 | **Heist chase** | The red thief rig sprinting off with a glowing Mythic orb; the owner chasing in the background, angry; pink shield fence glowing | "STEAL THEIR ORBS" | The steal hook; watch bounce closely, since it unlocks at Rebirth 1 |
| 4 | **Rich lab** | Over-the-shoulder view of a full row of pedestals with glowing orbs, cash balls streaming, a big in-game "$/s" number | "MAX YOUR LAB" | The tycoon/idle crowd (in-game cash only, never Robux) |
| 5 | **Event weather** (swap per update) | The lab under a purple Void Moon or a Meteor Shower, a Void or Celestial orb glowing in the centre | "VOID MOON" / "METEORS!" | Whether live events pull players; refresh it with each event update |

Make all five from real in-game captures (free camera plus Photopea glow and outline). Push saturation and bloom only a little, because the images have to match what players see. Put no Robux symbols, "FREE", fake buttons or update countdowns on any of them. Your shop's own "never" list already rules those out in-game, and the same thinking applies here. Keep the winner active in every later test, add 1-2 new concepts with each content update, and don't touch the set between updates.

**Title tag.** Copy the bracket-emoji pattern every leader uses ("[🌗] Steal a Brainrot", "[🐿️] Grow a Garden 🌶️") ([Steal a Brainrot](https://www.roblox.com/games/109983668079237/Steal-a-Brainrot); [Grow a Garden](https://www.roblox.com/games/126884695634066/Grow-a-Garden)). Use **"[🌈] Fusion Tycoon 🔮"** as the base, and swap the bracket emoji each update to match what's new: [⚡] for a Power Surge update, [🌙] for Void Moon, [☄️] for meteors, [🛡️] for a heist update. Put "OUT NOW: …" on the first line of the description, as Blox Fruits does. If you later want a name that states the loop the way the hits do, "Fuse & Steal Orbs" style naming is worth considering. Change the name and the icon at different times, though, so you can tell which change did what.

**What to measure.** In the Thumbnail Performance table, compare thumbnails on **QPTR and average playtime per qualified play together**. A thumbnail with high QPTR and short playtime is overselling (watch #3). In Analytics → Acquisition → Home Recommendations, track **play-through rate and first-play bounce (<60 s and 61-180 s) against the benchmark games**, plus play days per user. Your own AnalyticsKit funnel (Join → ClaimLab → FirstPull → FirstFuse) shows whether players who arrive actually reach the thing the art promised. If FirstFuse drops after you lead with "FUSE TO SECRET", the first minute needs work, not the art. Read results by update, not by day. Roblox says traffic peaks on Saturdays and dips as impressions grow. Since icon changes can't be tested, judge the icon on D1 retention and Search/Continue conversion over a clean 2-4 week window after each change.
